## Production principle

Use *one* frozen source pack, crosswalk, chapter contract and Style Bible. Separate draft generation from manuscript acceptance. AI is drafting assistance, not author of record, owner of decisions, or peer reviewer. Keep original author submissions and log source SHA, evidence claims and changes. Reject unsupported production, security, financial and lived-experience claims. Do not convert historical research or client-derived material into public copy without rights review.

## Complete book versus six-month minimum viable edition

**Preferred:** complete all 58 chapters at the frozen TOC page targets and complete required diagrams, decision pages, review, references, permissions and publisher files. **Six-month compression:** preserve all 58 numbers and mandated teaching obligations, but keep Chapters 46 and 51, 53–55, 58 exceptionally concise and move optional extended examples, worksheets, research dossiers and extra diagrams to a clearly identified companion *only if publisher approves*. Never defer the canonical Atlas (Chapter 23), its default text, foundational architecture (Chapters 10–22), main lifecycle account (24–34), operations (35–40), core playbooks (41–45, 47–50), glossary (56) or integrated worked evidence (57). Any actual contraction of the frozen chapter length/style requires explicit editorial exception; it is not assumed approved.

## Production sequence (drafting order, not TOC change)

- **Gate 0 / pilot:** Chapters 13 and 16, exact-source D-10/D-14/D-18 F1 specimens. Obtain genuine independent technical/editorial review; record hours, defects and first-submission quality. Do not run 5 concurrent drafts before passing.
- **Wave 1 / conceptual and authority spine:** Chapters 1–12 (excluding pilot 13), 14–22. Complete the canonical technical glossary before reuse in other waves. Finalise Atlas Chapter 23 only from frozen 30 records, not a rewrite.
- **Wave 2 / lifecycle and operations:** Chapters 24–40. Write stage chapters against exact prior decision definitions; separate stage entry/exit evidence from architectural enforcement. One complete Meridian journey must remain a synthetic example with factual constraints.
- **Wave 3 / practitioner experience:** Chapters 41–50. Make instructions and expected evidence actionable rather than repeating Wave 1 theory. Require platform/SRE/security reviewer for sensitive procedures.
- **Wave 4 / reference and integration:** Chapters 51–58. Use derived tables, exact sources, reproducible specimens, figure captions, reference list and index markers. Complete chapter-to-chapter transition edit and independent read-through.

Within each wave, draft only the approved cohort size. The current Phase 8 status does not establish a successful independent author trial or approval to scale to five chapters.

## Six-month scenario, scheduling and staffing

Frozen TOC allocates 448 chapter pages (about 478 total in proposal). This scenario budgets 2.1 human hours/page on high-risk/technical chapters and 1.55 hours/page elsewhere, including drafting and technical review; subtracts limited reuse of existing Chapter 16 draft; adds 22% rework/coordination and 75 integration/figure/rights hours. **Scenario: ~1,090 combined human hours** (not measured velocity). 26 weeks require **~42 combined productive human hours/week**. One architect at 15 hours/week offers **390 hours** and cannot meet that six-month full-manuscript target without drastically smaller publisher-authorised scope or additional people.

Suggested capacity: accountable lead author/architect 15 hours/week; technical writer/co-author 20 hours/week; independent specialist reviewer/editor 10 hours/week = **45 effective hours/week**, assuming review queues and scheduling work. These are distinct roles; AI-generated drafts do not eliminate human review. A 15–25% schedule reserve is advisable because trial metrics do not yet exist; the estimate already includes a 22% activity buffer but not publisher external decision time.

## Publisher package status and strategy

- **Apress / Springer Nature:** Existing 44-KB proposal draft + 28-KB competitive dossier provide substantial starting material, not a finished submission. Need verified legal author CV/credentials, complete synopses, 2 independently reviewed sample chapters (13/16) plus convincing opening sample (1 or 4), signed rights/permissions, source substantiation, realistic delivery schedule, scope/figures, explicit competing submissions and the publisher's form. Springer Nature encourages proposals and sample materials; follow the current commissioning editor's instructions rather than presuming a contract.
- **O'Reilly:** Possible if an editor supports the vendor-neutral practice-oriented reference. Requires sharply readable first chapters, concrete runnable or reviewable exercises, and stronger learning progression. A long compliance-like reference may be a weaker fit without editorial adaptation.
- **Addison-Wesley / Pearson:** Possible for a durable engineering methods/reference argument, requiring stronger originality demonstration, independent reader trials and consistent worked cases. Do not imply an open submission route without a confirmed commissioning contact.

**Recommended commercial move:** approach Apress with a credible revised proposal and quality-controlled sample chapters early. A publisher proposal can be submitted before the full 58-chapter manuscript is complete; this is not the same as a full manuscript submission. Preserve alternate publishers as options, subject to the proposal's exclusivity disclosure requirements.

## Six-month decision

A **proposal submission** within 4–6 weeks is plausible with verified human author facts and reviewed samples. A **complete 58-chapter manuscript delivered within six months** is a stretch commitment requiring approximately 42 productive combined human hours/week, an effective editorial lead and qualified reviewers, reliable source access and a successful pilot. At 15 hours/week solo, no credible six-month full-manuscript promise can be made; a 12–24-month or team-funded schedule is safer, subject to observed drafting/review velocity.
