## 2. Ownership and reader-facing explanation hierarchy

The key mechanism is a **single subject explanation home, multiple valid references**:

1. The frozen Atlas record is definitive for **decision default, deviation and reversibility**. All F1 text remains anchored to Chapter 23 and the catalogue.
2. A single architecture chapter is the **thematic explanation home** for the mechanism and responsibility. Chapters 10–22 do not take over another plane’s authority even when a request crosses multiple components.
3. A lifecycle chapter may explain **when** the decision applies and what evidence a stage must collect, but must link to the original mechanism.
4. A playbook may describe **how** to enact, verify and fail safely under that decision but cannot alter the default.
5. An operations chapter may describe **who supports** a capability or responds to failure and may not silently become its runtime PDP/PEP.
6. Appendices are reference and retrieval aids, not independently editable definitions.

The proposed thematic homes were selected from actual chapter titles, the Decision Catalogue’s chapter column and the Phase 3 traceability matrix; **they are not represented as explicit authoritative editorial approvals**. A human technical/editorial steward must examine all thirty assignments before mass authoring.

## 5. Controlled vocabulary authority

`VOCABULARY-AUTHORITY-24.csv` references the **24** Phase 3 terminology findings. It quotes or preserves the audit’s controlled meaning and source location. It does **not** claim that Chapter 56 already contains a fully approved term page. The required publishing behaviour is:

- First use: canonical capitalisation and owner link; later abbreviated use only when unambiguous.
- `Lifecycle (L)` means the execution/harness Structural Layer; `lifecycle stage` means Stage 0–10 enterprise delivery.
- `Gateway`, `Identity`, `Governance`, and `Verification` are distinct Control Planes; “security plane”, “evaluation plane”, and “semantic context plane” do not create new normative plane objects.
- `Approved`, `published`, `tested`, `reproduced`, `independently validated` and `retired` are distinct claims.
- A receipt must be explicitly graded: synthetic, signed, structural, observed and independently challenged are not interchangeable.
- A new alias is a proposed synonym requiring a source-owner review; a model may not make up a short name for a canonical owner.

## 6. Evidence traceability and claim semantics

**Five very different propositions must remain different fields:**

| Proposition | Acceptable proof | Insufficient substitute |
|---|---|---|
| REFERENCED | Exact source locator, identifier and stable revision | A model says it remembers a source |
| IMPLEMENTED | Actual code/configuration and bounded behaviour at a pinned revision | A design diagram |
| TESTED | Input, environment, expected and observed output, run reference and timestamp | A test definition without execution |
| REPRODUCED | Independent execution of the same test recipe with matching observable results | Original author repeating an assertion |
| INDEPENDENTLY VALIDATED | Qualified non-author review, recorded verdict, challenge and observed result | A second AI reviewing the first AI |
| APPROVED | Qualified human authority, exact revision, scope and conditions | A PR comment, a model verdict or file presence |
| PUBLISHED | Release or publisher record, exact revision and audience | Approved draft not released |

Fifteen concrete evidence and authority categories are defined in `EVIDENCE-TAXONOMY-15.csv`. It distinguishes a source citation from an implementation assertion; a policy decision from enforcement; per-tool action outcome from a release gate; synthetic narrative from observed operations; and an AI recommendation from a human approval. A source-linked graph edge `references` must never be converted automatically to `implements`, `tested_by`, `validates`, or `approved_by`.

Recommended trace record for authoring (derived mapping only, not new governance): `{claim_id, chapter_id, source_id, source_sha256, decision_ids, evidence_class, environment, observation_status, review_status, approver_ref, related_meridian_event_ids, limitations}`. Omit undefined fields rather than fabricate values. No additional fixture IDs are minted.

## 7. Structural consistency rules for GPT, Claude, Codex and human authors

The existing Multi-AI Contributor Operating Model remains the authority. Phase 4 adds **content-specific review assertions**, not another agent hierarchy or workflow:

1. **Preflight:** pin approved chapter scope, Style Bible version, Atlas/catalogue revision, exact Meridian fixture checksum and all related definitions. Do not draft on the basis of a conversational recollection alone.
2. **Scope assertion:** match each claim to the chapter’s `authority_lane`; a chapter may reference other homes but may not rewrite them.
3. **Decision literal preservation:** require exact D-## ID, title, default and exception text in Chapter 23; supporting chapters paraphrase faithfully with link and no new normative text.
4. **Vocabulary lint:** check canonical capitalisation, prohibited aliases and any deprecated legacy layer wording; flag quotations as historical context rather than silently rewriting them.
5. **Meridian provenance lint:** require exact frozen event and evidence IDs; identify fictional/synthetic grade; prohibit fabricated signing, production claims, financial side effects, approved autonomy or extra events.
6. **Evidence-status lint:** forbid `validated` or `approved` unless a real test run or human approval source exists. An automated test success only proves what the test actually checks.
7. **Cross-artefact guard:** verify chapter→decision→source→evidence and lifecycle/playbook references; report disjoint topic-home assignments before review.
8. **Review separation:** peer AI may flag problems and propose patches; security, canonical authoring status and final release authority remain human-only.
9. **Failure output:** ambiguous owner, inconsistent source, missing evidence, unknown approval or mismatch of the Style Bible with TOC must return BLOCKED/REQUIRES_REVIEW, not invented consensus.

An AI may produce a chapter draft under an assigned work item only. Two AI authors may coordinate on separate chapters but must not have overlapping write authority to a frozen source. No automatic merge or graduation to publication is authorised by this audit.

## 10. Authoritative reconciliation boundary and final verdict

**This is a single derived authoring model, not a competing architectural authority.** The source artifacts remain frozen. A metadata disagreement is reported as an open editorial reconciliation item. The authoring matrix cannot grant access, amend an Atlas default, authorise publication or manufacture evidence. The model can be promoted as an accepted crosswalk only after a responsible human steward signs off the exact source-revision manifest and the crosswalk exceptions are resolved using existing change/review authority.

**GO:** Continue bounded preparation and sample drafting of Chapters 13/16 and exact Atlas F1 specimen comparisons.  
**CONDITIONAL GO:** Pilot publication workflows with human review on a pinned, internally consistent version of the crosswalk.  
**NO GO:** Unsupervised or large-scale concurrent production of the 57 remaining chapters today.

**Shortest action:** classify the 26 Atlas–TOC link asymmetries; approve the chapter subject homes and vocabulary errata as editorial metadata; run one blind two-author D-10/D-14/D-18 pilot; adopt only the crosswalk version that the independent reviewers can reproduce. No architectural redesign, extra chapters or new Atlas IDs are necessary.
