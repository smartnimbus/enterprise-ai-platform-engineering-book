# Apress Acquisition Competitive Dossier — Version 2

**Proposed book:** *The Enterprise AI Agent Fabric: Designing, Governing and Operating AI Agents at Scale*  
**Prepared:** 25 November 2026  
**Purpose:** Publisher-facing comparison for an Apress acquisitions conversation. The comparison describes reader needs and distinctive proposed learning outcomes. It does not suggest that competing authors omit all overlapping concepts.

## Market position in one paragraph

This book competes in a well-served and developing market: enterprise AI adoption, platform engineering, agent identity, secure autonomy and AI systems engineering. Its organising unit is not an SDK, component list or policy catalogue. It is a **defensible enterprise decision** linked to a named owner, an enforcement or operating mechanism, a verification condition and an inspectable evidence artefact. The distinction is primarily **pedagogical integration and reference usability**. Individual techniques—including gateways, control planes, identity delegation, evaluation, platform products and risk governance—are established practices described by other books.

## Acquisition positioning: credible distinctions

**Compared with governance-led books.** The book neither displaces governance nor claims to invent non-bypassability. It asks who makes each consequential business and technical decision, which control enforces it, what recorded evidence demonstrates that operation, and how the decision changes during release, incident, cost review and retirement. Chapters 13 and 16 demonstrate distinct Context and Gateway responsibilities; the Atlas makes those choices discoverable.

**Compared with platform-building books.** Building and operating platforms, developer enablement, CI/CD, evaluation and observability have extensive existing coverage—particularly in *Building AI Agent Platforms*. This book asks the additional enterprise choice question: under what circumstances should multiple use cases share this capability, who remains accountable for business outcomes, and what would justify a different design? It is deliberately not a step-by-step implementation guide to one software stack.

**Compared with identity specialists.** Agent identities and delegated authority are essential specialist subjects, not proprietary inventions. The Fabric reference explains how identity assertions are evaluated at an authorised action boundary, combined with contextual provenance, tested against bypass, and linked to incident evidence. *AI Identities* remains a legitimate deeper identity companion.

**Compared with systems engineering.** Software architecture, quality attributes, testability and DevOps are already treated in *Engineering AI Systems*. The proposed book narrows its specific example to enterprise portfolios of autonomous or agent-assisted workloads and their shared operating obligations. The emphasis is on actionable cross-team decisions rather than a replacement for general AI systems engineering.

## A worked positioning example: decision to evidence

An AI agent retrieves conflicting customer-product records and proposes an account-changing tool call. The **Context** responsibility preserves provenance and marks the business claim unresolved; it does not choose a convenient source as truth. The **Identity** responsibility binds the actor and any delegation to permitted scopes. The **Gateway** evaluates whether the proposed mutable action is authorised at the action boundary and prevents an unauthorised call from proceeding. The **Verification** responsibility later judges whether the candidate version meets declared assertions. Observability and retained records support reconstruction. In the **synthetic Meridian case**, the refund-changing action is denied and no external mutation occurs. A corresponding Atlas page makes the decision default and exception criteria inspectable; a playbook turns that contract into a review procedure. This is the reader experience the samples must show, not evidence of a deployed production system.

## Related reading beyond the direct comparison

Platform engineering, system architecture, zero-trust, DevSecOps, SRE, software assurance and AI evaluation books are complementary sources. Their contributions should be acknowledged in the manuscript wherever relevant. No claim is made that these fields lack decision records, ownership patterns, retirement practice or verification engineering.

## Editorial positioning guardrails

- **Do claim:** a consistent decision-to-evidence teaching structure spanning strategic funding, responsibility, action enforcement, verification, economics, operations and retirement.
- **Do claim:** vendor neutrality in the *method* and deliberately cross-technology examples, subject to the final manuscript.
- **Do not claim:** the first agent fabric, the first AI control-plane model, a unique gateway technology, or a monopoly on agent governance.
- **Do not claim:** direct sales demand or competitor topic absence without independent evidence.
- **Do not claim:** a synthetic Meridian result is a field-validated production deployment.
- **Do not present:** catalogue-based prominence judgments as measured percentages of textual overlap.
