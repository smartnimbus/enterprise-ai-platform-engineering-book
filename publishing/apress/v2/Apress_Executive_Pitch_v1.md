# The Enterprise AI Agent Fabric
## Apress Executive Acquisition Pitch

**Proposed subtitle:** *Designing, Governing and Operating AI Agents at Scale*  
**Professional audience:** Enterprise architects and AI platform leads; security and operations teams  
**Proposed extent:** Approximately 478 pages including front matter and index; 58 chapters in eight parts

### A clear problem after the agent pilot

Building an AI agent is an application problem; operating many agents safely is a platform engineering problem. The first successful enterprise agent often creates the conditions for the next hard choices: when should a capability be shared, who owns its outcomes, who authorises its tool calls, what happens when its context conflicts, what evidence proves that it behaved as intended, and when should the system be retired?

Most organisations do not need a new central platform merely because they have built one capable agent. But as portfolios grow, duplicated connectors, inconsistent policies and incomplete operational ownership create escalating cost and risk. An architect or platform leader needs to make bounded, defendable choices—not simply adopt a framework or a checklist of products.

### A book organised around decisions that can be checked

*The Enterprise AI Agent Fabric* offers a vendor-neutral reference in which every major design choice connects to an accountable owner, a real enforcement or operating mechanism, a test, and a concrete record. Its **Decision Atlas** provides thirty consistently structured one-page decisions. A four-layer, eight-control-plane reference explains which technical responsibilities must be assigned; an eleven-stage lifecycle and ten practitioner playbooks show how the choices are made, revisited and unwound.

The organising method—**decision-to-evidence engineering**—is the book's chief practical distinction. It does not claim to invent agent fabrics, control planes, security gateways, platform engineering or governance. It shows readers how to connect these established disciplines so that a decision survives design review, release, incident, finance review and retirement.

### What makes the technical content concrete

Two example chapters contrast the boundaries that agent-platform teams regularly blur. **Context (Chapter 13)** explains how evidence, retrieval and memory preserve provenance and report unresolved conflict without creating business truth. **Gateway Control Plane (Chapter 16)** explains how an agent's proposed consequential action is admitted or denied against delegated authority and policy, and how bypass must be tested. The recurring fictional Meridian Financial case records a denied refund-changing action, conflicting source information and a failed release assertion, with all evidence clearly labelled synthetic.

An architect reading the book can finish an investment decision and its alternatives; a platform lead can define a reusable service contract; a security reviewer can require a negative action test and inspect its receipt; an operator can reconstruct the incident or retire the use case. The result is an applied professional reference, not a single-vendor programming tutorial.

### Why Apress

The Apress catalogue already speaks to enterprise AI governance, agent adoption, identity and architecture. This proposal complements those subjects by making *the reviewable enterprise decision*—and the evidence that confirms or disproves it—the persistent unit of teaching. Comparable books have genuine overlapping coverage. This book aims to earn its position through a connected, reusable format extending from deciding not to build a shared service to documenting its eventual withdrawal.
