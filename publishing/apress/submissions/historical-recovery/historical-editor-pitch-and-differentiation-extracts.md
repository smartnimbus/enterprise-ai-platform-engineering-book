## 6. Publisher-facing writing to use instead of internal process prose

**Two-sentence editor pitch**

> Organisations are learning to build AI agents faster than they can decide who may authorise their actions, how competing evidence should be reconciled, and when shared infrastructure is worth its cost. *The Enterprise AI Agent Fabric* gives architects and platform leaders 30 consistent decision records and practical operating artefacts to make those choices explicit—from the first platform investment decision to eventual retirement.

**Distinctive format paragraph**

> The book is organised around a repeatable decision-to-evidence method. A decision records its default, exceptions, accountable owner and reversal conditions; accompanying technical chapters show the relevant system boundary; and worked operational artefacts show what a reviewer can verify. A fictional running case illustrates consequences without claiming to be production deployment evidence.

**Why this book instead of neighbouring titles**

> Existing agent engineering, platform engineering and AI governance books cover important portions of this problem. This reference differs in its unit of instruction: a complete enterprise decision with an owner, a justified alternative, an operational checkpoint and a resulting artefact. Its claim is editorial integration and practical usability, not invention of the technologies or absence of competing coverage.

## 3. Dossier reality check and competitive correction

The November 9 dossier transparently states that 28 books were compared from public catalogue summaries. That is a valid discovery tool, **not evidence that an editor can assign precise overlap percentages or assert a whole topic is absent**. Replace percentage/0–3 charts in the publisher-facing comparison with a six-to-seven-title qualitative grid: reader, overlapping strengths, *what is still left for this book*, and a specific finished sample that demonstrates it. Retain numerical analysis as internal working notes.

- **Most serious Apress overlaps:** *Governance-First AI* (policies and production architecture), *Transformation Enterprise Architecture with Agentic AI* (EA/agent transformation), *Agentic AI in Enterprise* (broad enterprise adoption). These titles cannot be dismissed as superficial.
- **Strongest external competitive threat:** O’Reilly's *Building AI Agent Platforms*. Its accessible early-release contents address platform as a product, runtimes, self-service, evaluation, observability, governance, economics and resilience. The 2027 print-date claim alone is misleading if early-release reading is already available. Do not position this proposal as the first or only comprehensive platform book.
- **Adjacent technical comparables:** *AI Identities* (dedicated identity treatment), *AI Engineering* (evaluations and AI application systems), and Addison-Wesley's *Engineering AI Systems: Architecture and DevOps Essentials*. They narrow any claim of unique technical content.
- **Defensible narrow differentiation:** repeatable decision *record* → explicit default/reversal criterion → named owner/control → observed or explicitly synthetic *evidence* → operating playbook → retirement. This combination must be materially demonstrated within actual pages, not only described.
- **New evidence needed:** dated source verification for top direct titles; reader interviews and technical reactions to finished samples; a worked 'no shared Fabric' choice; a source-exact F1 decision with a matching Gateway/Context test and adverse outcome.

## The acquisition proposition

Enterprises are moving beyond agent demonstrations into portfolios of assistants, workflows and autonomous actors that read protected information and may act on business systems. The technical question is no longer merely how to build one capable agent. It is how to choose which capabilities should be shared, who is allowed to delegate consequential actions, how to recognise unresolved business context, and what evidence justifies releasing, scaling, changing or retiring a service.

*The Enterprise AI Agent Fabric* gives technology leaders a repeatable way to make and defend those decisions. It combines a vendor-neutral architectural reference with a **30-decision Atlas**, explicitly assigned responsibilities, measurable enforcement and verification conditions, and practical records that can be used in architecture reviews, product onboarding, security approval, operations and eventual exit. It treats 'do not build a shared Fabric' as a valid result when the economics or service boundaries do not support centralisation.

The book's distinctive teaching pattern is **decision-to-evidence engineering**: for each material enterprise choice, record the default and available alternatives, name the accountable owner, identify the actual enforcement or operating point, define the test or review needed, and specify what artefact would support acceptance. The method connects subjects frequently taught separately—platform ownership, agent identity, context provenance, security, evaluation, FinOps and retirement—without claiming to invent any of them.

Its intended readers are practitioners responsible for more than a single agent demo. They need an architecture they can explain to stakeholders and an operating method they can use to say *yes*, *not yet* or *no* to a proposed workload. The book is designed to be reopened at each such decision, rather than read once and discarded.

## Commercial fit and competitive context

Apress serves working technologists and enterprise practitioners. The proposed book is designed as a durable professional reference rather than a fast-moving catalogue of model APIs. Its immediate peers include Apress books on governance, enterprise agent adoption and AI identities, plus O’Reilly books addressing AI agent platforms and agent governance, and broader AI systems engineering literature.

The book does **not** claim that competitors omit evaluation, security, identity or operational controls. *Building AI Agent Platforms*, for example, addresses substantial platform implementation and product concerns; *AI Agent Governance* addresses agent governance in depth. The proposed difference is the decision-centric structure connecting business justification to named enforcement, verifiable evidence, cost and eventual withdrawal. The accompanying **Apress Acquisition Competitive Dossier v2** supplies a qualitative buyer comparison and publisher-catalogue links.
